# Nakshatra — The Sky Weavers

**A hand-crafted 3D sky lab for curious minds (Classes 6–9). Seven lanterns. One real sky. No forced path.**

Step onto a hilltop in India at night. The stars above you are real — 8,879 of them, placed from the HYG catalogue. Seven paper lanterns are waiting, and every one is open from the first second. Each one opens with a short written **Sky Note** — the idea in plain language first, then the thing you can move, break and rebuild.

- **The Moon's Mask** — drag the Moon around Earth and watch the phase change in a live “view from Earth”.
- **Thread the Stars** — find Saptarishi, Mriga, Sharmishtha, Vrishchika and Hamsa in the real sky.
- **The Long Road** — travel from the Sun to Neptune. See true distances in light-minutes and true sizes beside Earth.
- **The Turning Year** — tilt the Earth, find the solstices, and make the seasons disappear.
- **Solar Storm Lab** — twist the Sun's magnetic field, launch a flare and race a CME to Earth. Learn what auroras, satellites and space-weather forecasts have to do with each other.
- **Black Hole Lab** — lower a probe toward an event horizon and watch its clock slow. Built around the real physics behind the Event Horizon Telescope's image of M87*, with no movie myths.
- **Sky Riddles** — fourteen friendly questions with an explanation after every answer. Use them before and after a lesson.
- **Field Guide to the Universe** — readable cards on nebulae, supernovae, pulsars, galaxies, gravitational waves and exoplanets.
- **Sky Atlas** — the whole sky at 13°N, with 88 constellations, 28 nakshatras, star names and a time slider.

When all seven lanterns are lit, the town releases its lanterns and you can keep a **Sky Weaver certificate** with your name.

**New in this edition: Seminar mode.** A twelve-slide presenter walkthrough is built into the same game — speaker notes, a rehearsal timer, auto-advance, a pre-flight check, demo-state controls and one-click jumps into every live exhibit. Open it from the title screen, or add `?scene=seminar` to the URL. The full playbook is in the download and in the project documentation.

Everything is **free-choice, offline, and phone-friendly**. Kannada key terms sit alongside English. No accounts, no ads, no tracking, no internet needed after loading.

> Made for a school community project, built as a real thing a classroom can keep. Facts are checked against NASA and NCERT/State syllabus topics; the numbers are real, while the orrery is drawn compressed so all eight planets can share a screen.

**Controls:** mouse or touch. Drag to look, tap stars and planets, drag the Moon, use the sliders. Portrait is the primary phone layout; landscape works too. In Seminar mode: arrows/space to move, `N` notes, `D` checks, `F` fullscreen, `I` enlarge a figure.
